# Find Your Offer: the verdict

Read by the skill. This is part 6. Run it in the order below. Four questions at most: one check per top candidate, the camera question, and the sentence.

The `pending` ids for this part are `check_1`, `check_2` (or `niche` on Route C), `camera` and `sentence`. Record each reply under its id like any other answer.

## 1. The edge card

Build three lines from the "what to listen for" notes. Each line is something true about this person that most people cannot say. Their words, their facts. If you only have two, print two. If the interview was thin, print what there is and do not dress it up.

The closing line depends on what you have:
- Three strong lines: "Most people cannot say those three things. You can."
- Thin: "That is thin. Fine. Thin is where most people start, and it is why the next step is talking to buyers."

## The first verdict card

Everything up to the first question goes on one card, stacked in this order. This is the only card that carries this much.

```
FIND YOUR OFFER   ■■■■■■■■■■■■■■□□   Part 6 of 6: THE VERDICT

WHAT I HEARD: THE MARKET
  • <their words>
  • <their words>

YOUR EDGE, {NAME}
  1. <an unusual mix, a hard thing done, or a result with proof>
  2. <...>
  3. <...>
<the closing line>

WHAT YOU COULD SELL
  1. <who> | <problem> | <what you would do>   (both sides)
  2. <who> | <problem> | <what you would do>   (profession)

<the first verdict question: quick check 1, or on Route C the niche pick>
```

No candidates? Replace the WHAT YOU COULD SELL block with one line: "Nothing here is ready to sell yet. Good. That makes the next step simple." 

## 2. The candidates

A candidate is one line with three parts: **who** has the problem, **what** the problem is, **what you would do** about it.

- Every part comes from an answer. You fill in nothing.
- Build up to three. One where a life answer and a market answer point at the same thing comes first: that is "both sides", and it is the strongest.
- It must be a thing done for someone else. "Get fitter" is not a candidate.
- No real candidates? Do not force one. Go to step 4 with none.

Show them on the first verdict card, numbered, strongest first, with where each came from (pain, profession, passion, the market, or both sides). The top two are the first two on that list.

## 3. The quick check (top two only)

One card per candidate that exists: two candidates get two checks, one gets one, none gets none. Proof only counts when it is proof of this candidate, for this kind of buyer or close to it.

One card per candidate. Three yes or no lines, answered together in one reply:

```
QUICK CHECK: <candidate, short>

Answer all three. Yes or no, then the detail.

   1  BUYERS  Name five real people or businesses with this problem.
   2  PROOF   Have you done this once, for you or anyone, with something to show?
   3  READY   Could you deliver it in the next 14 days with what you know today?
```

Score 1 or 0 each. Never round up.
- BUYERS scores 1 for four or more real names. A type of person ("dentists") is not a name.
- PROOF scores 1 when they name the time and the thing they can show.
- READY scores 1 for a yes with no course, tool or licence still to get.

Keep the names. They go on the result page.

Tie? Ask nothing more. The one that came from "both sides" wins. Still tied: the one closer to their FORCED pick in pass_2.

## 4. The route

| Route | When | What they get |
|---|---|---|
| **A. You have an edge** | The best candidate scores 2 or 3 | A service built on their own talent |
| **B. No clear edge yet** | There are candidates but the best scores 0 or 1, or there are skills and no candidate | The simplest model, pointed at a niche they can reach |
| **C. Starting from zero** | No candidate, and nothing in pain, profession or passion to build on | One of five niches plus the simplest model |

Say which route on the next card, as a block above that card's question. Never as bad news. Route B and Route C are how most people start.

```
YOUR ROUTE: <A. You have an edge / B. The simplest model / C. Start with a niche>
  <one line: what that means for them>
```

### Route A
The service is their top candidate. Their backup is the second one.

### Route B: the simplest model
Pick one of two, from their answer to mkt_4:

- **AI implementation** (mkt_4 was A or B). "You set up AI tools inside a small business so a job that took hours takes minutes. You do not need to invent anything. You need to be one step ahead of the owner."
- **Lead generation** (mkt_4 was C or D). "You find a business its next customers. You do not need to be an expert in their trade. You need a list, a message and volume."

Point it at the group from mkt_1 they know best. If mkt_1 was G (nobody), use the group they named in mkt_2 and keep it marked as a guess. Use any skill from the interview as the angle (a bookkeeper doing lead generation for accountants has a reason to be believed).

### Route C: pick a niche
Print the five and make them choose one. Their warm network decides: a group they ticked on mkt_1 beats a group they like the sound of.

```
PICK ONE. Which of these do you already know people in?

   A  Trades and home services
   B  Clinics (dentists, physios, skin clinics)
   C  Professional services (accountants, lawyers, advisers)
   D  Agencies and recruiters
   E  Coaches, consultants and course creators
```

If they know nobody in any of them: "Then pick the one you would happily talk to for an hour." Model is lead generation, or AI implementation if mkt_4 was A or B.

On Route C there are no quick checks, so the verdict is three questions: the niche, the camera, the sentence.

## 5. The research method

Every route gets this. Say this first, on the card:

"You do not need a finished offer. You need a rough idea and real conversations. You are going to go and ask the market what its problems are, and build the offer out of what you hear."

Then ask:

```
How do you feel about being on camera?

   A  Fine. Put me on.
   B  Nervous, but I will do it.
   C  No. Not a chance.
```

- A or B: the **podcast method**.
- C: the **white paper method**.

### The podcast method
You start a small interview show for your niche. You invite the people you want as clients to be guests. You record a 20 to 30 minute call on Riverside or Zoom. You cut it into short clips and give them the clips to post on LinkedIn and social. That is what they get out of it: free content with their face on it.

What you get: half an hour of a buyer telling you their problems in their own words, and a relationship.

Invite, filled with their niche:
"Hi {first name}, I am starting a short interview show for {niche}. I would love to have you on for 20 minutes to talk about how you {what they do}. I will cut it into short clips you can post. Open to it?"

### The white paper method
For people who will not go on camera. You are writing a short report on the biggest problems in your niche. You interview people for it. Nothing is recorded. In return they get the finished report before anyone else. You take notes, and AI turns the notes into a clean PDF.

What you get: the same thing. Buyers telling you their problems in their own words.

Invite, filled with their niche:
"Hi {first name}, I am writing a short report on the biggest problems {niche} are dealing with around {area}. Could I ask you five questions? 15 minutes, not recorded. You get the finished report first. Open to it?"

### Filling the blanks
- `{niche}`: the buyer group, in their words.
- `{area}`: the problem area in two to five words, taken from mkt_2 or mkt_5 ("getting paid on time", "finding work in winter"). If it reads badly in the sentence, shorten it. Do not swap in your own idea.
- `{what they do}`: the buyer's own work around that area, in plain words.
- `{first name}`: leave it as `[first name]`. It is the name of whoever they send it to.

### Who to ask
1. **Warm first.** The names from mkt_1, pain_2 and the quick check. People who already know them say yes.
2. **Then cold.** The same kind of person, strangers, with the same invite.

### What to ask on the call
1. What does a normal week look like for you?
2. What is the biggest headache in {area} right now?
3. What have you tried to fix it? What happened?
4. What is it costing you: time, money, customers?
5. If it was fixed tomorrow, what would change?
6. Who else should I talk to?

Do not pitch on these calls. Ask and listen.

### The tally
After every call, write down the three biggest problems in their exact words. After a handful of calls the same words and the same problems start to repeat. Circle them. That is the offer. Build the answer to the problem you keep hearing.

This is what Sam Ubbins did. It is what Jordan did. It is what to do when you do not know where to start.

## 6. The sentence

Write it from their answers and show it. They change any word that is not how they would say it. A reply like "yes" or "keep it" keeps yours. Anything else is their edit: use their wording. Save the final one to the top-level `sentence`.

- Route A: "I help {who} get {outcome} using {what I am good at}."
- Route B and C: "I help {niche} with {lead generation or AI}, and I am interviewing {niche} to find out what they need most." This one is meant to be rough. Tell them it is rough on purpose.

## 7. The result page

Fill from `answers.json`. Leave a line out when they gave nothing for it. Never fill a gap yourself. Keep every bullet to one line.

- **Talents** are what is unusual about them (the edge lines). **Skills** are things they can do for someone. Do not print the same line in both.
- **Why you** is the single strongest reason, one line, not a list.
- **Backup:** Route A, the second candidate as one tidy line. Route B, the second candidate if there is one, else the other model. Route C, the other model.
- **Start with these people:** every real person they named, a first name or a business name or "my uncle the roofer". None at all? Print: "No names yet. Your first job: write down five {niche} you could reach with one message."
- When the result is written, set `pending` to an empty string.
